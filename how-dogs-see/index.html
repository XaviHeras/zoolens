How do dogs see? Try it on your own photo

Dogs see blues and yellows, but reds and greens look alike to them. Fine detail looks softer: about 20/75, where people with good sight see 20/20. And they are quick to notice movement.

Question 1

What colours can dogs see?

Blues and yellows; reds and greens look alike to them. They don’t see in black and white: dogs have two kinds of colour cones, where most people have three. Drag the line to compare.

Question 2

Which toy colour is easiest for a dog to see?

On grass, a red ball almost disappears for a dog, because red and green look alike. A blue or yellow toy is likely to stand out more.

Question 3

How sharp is a dog’s sight?

About 20/75: a dog has to be 20 feet (6 m) away to make out what you can make out from 75 feet (23 m). The picture zooms in on the face so you can see the softer detail.

Do dogs notice movement?

Yes. Dogs are good at seeing what moves. In the app, moving things stay sharp and still things look a little dull; with your camera, this page does the same. In a still photo nothing moves, so the whole picture shows the still view.

How do dogs see at night?

Better than we do, though no one knows exactly how much. Their retina has lots of rods, the cells that work in dim light, and behind it a reflective layer, the tapetum lucidum: it gives the retina a second chance to catch the light, and it’s what makes their eyes shine in flash photos. The Dog view on this page doesn’t simulate the dark; in the app, the Cat view does open the shadows into a night view.

How this works

The demo uses the same colour maths as Zoolens. The dog’s colours come from one setting of a general colour-vision model (Machado and others, 2009): the green-blind (deuteranopia) setting, applied in linear light. The blur comes from the 20/75 figure and is exaggerated 3.5 times so it shows on a screen. Everything happens in this tab: once the demo has started, you can turn on airplane mode and it still works.
