Cat · Free

A cat’s eyes: softer detail, quieter colour

Cats mix up red and green too, and fine detail looks softer to them than to us. In the app, shadows open up into a blue-grey night view with a fine grain.

The puppy photo: your eyes on the left; on the right, the Cat effect, softer and with quieter colour.

Your eyesBee Free

Bee · Free

A bee’s eyes: flowers in false colour

Bees see ultraviolet, which a phone camera can’t. Zoolens guesses it from ordinary colour and shows a false-colour view through a honeycomb of facets: greens turn red-orange and red almost disappears.

The colours are a convention, not what a bee feels. Blur is exaggerated so it shows on a screen.

Meadow flowers: your eyes on the left; on the right, the Bee effect, a blurry honeycomb in false colour.

Mantis shrimp · Premium

A mantis shrimp’s eyes: colour in bold bands

It has 12 kinds of colour receptor, yet tells close colours apart worse than we do: it may recognise colours rather than compare them. The app snaps colours into bold, flat bands. Polarised light and far ultraviolet can’t be shown.

Premium: in the Sea pack, or free once a day as your premium photo or 10-second video.

Live, in your camera

Point your iPhone at anything and the view changes live. Swipe to change eyes. Take photos and videos with the effect; they’re saved to your Photos.

The app works on the live camera; it doesn’t edit photos from your library. The demo above lets you try the idea on a photo.

The mark on free photos

Free photos and videos carry a mark: the name of the view in the top-left corner, like ‘Dog vision · Zoolens · see like an animal’, and a small ‘Zoolens’ in the bottom-right.

The mark isn’t tiny: its title is about a twentieth of the photo’s width, so people can read which view it is.

A pack removes it for its five animals. Any plan removes it everywhere. In the app, the button says ‘Remove watermark with Premium’.
